# Tabs (issue #166, child of toolkit epic #72)

**Status:** executed.

**Date:** 2026-08-10

## What shipped

`components.Tabs` + `components.TabItem` in `gooey/components`, the
`<Tabs>`/`<Tab>` markup builders, a `docs/markup-reference.md` entry,
damage-count contract tests, and the adoption of the component by the
customer that asked for it — `examples/kanbandemo`'s bottom panel, whose
hand-rolled switcher explicitly noted "gooey has no Tab component yet".
`cmd/reader`'s panes are contract surface and were left alone.

```xml
<Tabs Selected="{{.Tab}}">
  <Tab Header="mcp"><Border …/></Tab>
  <Tab Header="log"><LogPanel …/></Tab>
</Tabs>
```

## The design in one sentence

The strip is `Segmented` grown a body, and the body is the bindable-
Visibility machinery — nothing about switching was invented here.

## The decisions

### Switching is a visibility flip, never a structural change

Every page's content is a permanent child. `ChildComponents()` binds
each page's `Layout` with `BindVisibilityFunc(selected == me)` before
the tree composes (the Composer's build walk calls it, and its
`armVisibility` runs after, so the observers arm on the first build).
A `Set` on `Selected` then rides issue #12's machinery end to end: the
observer schedules the frame, layout collapses the outgoing page to
zero, the sweep erases its cells and restores what was under them, the
incoming page repaints because becoming visible dirties it, and the
strip repaints because its `Render` read `Selected`.

The damage pin, asserted by contract tests: **a switch with the Tabs as
composition root paints exactly 3 nodes** — strip, outgoing (which
executes for its bounds change but renders nothing), incoming. Inside a
stack it is 4, the extra being the ancestor restore over the vacated
rect — the same one repaint the literal visibility sweep costs (count
parity with `markup/visibility_test.go`). An idle frame paints 0; a
header change or hover paints 1; a focus move paints 2.

`Collapsed` (not `Hidden`) is what makes the rest free: hidden pages
drop out of layout, focus order (`FocusManager.reachable`) and
hit-testing without a line of Tabs code.

Because the Tabs owns those bindings, a `Visibility` attribute on a
page root in markup is a **load error** ("the Tabs owns it") rather
than a binding that would be silently replaced.

### Selected is an int, not a key

`<Tab Header=…>` might suggest keying the selection by header string.
It is a bound `*prop.Property[int]` instead:

- **Precedent.** `Segmented.Selected` and `ItemsView.Selected` are both
  bound ints; a viewmodel that has driven either drives this.
- **No reflection, no new registry.** `boundProp[int]` already exists;
  a key would need name-uniqueness validation and a lookup table.
- **Headers are not identities.** `Header` is itself bindable — a
  header showing a live count is not a stable key to select by.

Out-of-range values clamp on read (the `Segmented` rule), so a
viewmodel ahead of a shorter tab list still shows the last page.
`Selected` is optional in markup; absent, the control keeps its own
source starting at 0.

### The keyboard: rocker arrows on the strip, ctrl+pgup/pgdn in the subtree

The strip is one focus stop. While focused, `←`/`→` follow the rocker
rule — consumed only when the selection moves, so an end-of-travel
arrow bubbles out to spatial focus navigation — and `home`/`end` jump.

`ctrl+pgup`/`ctrl+pgdn` (the tmux/browser convention) cycle with wrap
and are handled in `Tabs.HandleKey` **without** checking focus: they
reach the Tabs by bubbling from whatever descendant holds focus. The
decision recorded here: their scope is the **Tabs subtree**, not the
page. That is the same scoping a `KeyBinding` declared on a container
gets, it means nested Tabs cannot fight over the gesture, and it needs
no new routing seam. A page that wants them truly global declares its
own `KeyBinding`s (kanbandemo keeps `ctrl+t` for exactly this reason).

### Focus is rescued from the outgoing page

Selecting away from a page whose descendant holds focus would strand
focus on a collapsed component — keys would still bubble from it, but
`tab` order and the screen would disagree about where the keyboard is.
`Tabs` implements `gooey.FocusHost` (the seam ButtonBar introduced) and
`Select` moves focus to the strip when the previously-focused component
was inside the outgoing page. Outside a composed tree (`mgr == nil`)
the rescue is skipped.

### The control sizes to its active page

`Measure` returns strip + the measured active page (collapsed pages
measure zero through the ordinary `MeasureChild` path). The
alternative — measuring hidden pages at natural size for a constant
footprint — would have to bypass `MeasureChild`'s collapse short-
circuit and with it margins and explicit sizes. Recorded consequence:
pages of different heights move the strip on switch; authors who want a
stable footprint give the pages equal explicit `Height`s, which is what
kanbandemo does (both pages `Height="12"`).

### Wheel steps, click selects, both strip-only

`WheelUp`/`WheelDown` over the strip row step the selection without
wrapping, and an end-of-travel scroll is **not** consumed (the rocker
rule again — it stays available to an enclosing scroller). A click on a
header selects it; the separator column belongs to neither header; the
content area's clicks are the page's business. Hover and disable follow
wave 1: hover underlines the strip, an absent `Changed` is inert, a
false `CanExecute` paints dim and refuses everything, and the condition
is read while painting so the flip repaints exactly the strip.

## Markup contract

`<Tabs>` takes `Selected` (optional int binding), `Changed`, `Style`,
`Name`, non-visual attachments, and `<Tab>` children — a visual
non-`<Tab>` child is a load error, as is `<Tab>` anywhere else, a
missing `Header`, a `<Tab>` without exactly one content child, an empty
`<Tabs>`, and a page root binding its own `Visibility`.

## Not here

Mnemonic headers (`_1`/alt+1 — the MenuBar mnemonic seam exists and a
follow-up can reuse it), closeable/overflowing strips, vertical strips,
and runtime tab add/remove (the visibility bindings are made once; a
dynamic tab list needs the `Dynamic`/`InvalidateStructure` path and a
re-bind, its own change).
