One more idea before anything gets built. You don't write the layout in code. You write it in a separate file that describes what goes where — a panel, some text inside it, a line under that. If you've seen HTML, it will look familiar. That's it on the left. And on the right is the same file. Not a picture of what it would look like, and not a copy I kept in step by hand. The right pane is that exact file, loaded and running. And look at what isn't in it. There's no instruction to update, nothing that watches anything, nothing that redraws on a schedule. It's a description of a panel with a number in it. The number is changing because that line reads it, and reading it is the only thing that has to happen. And that file is live. You can edit it while the program is running, save it, and watch the screen change. No restart. No rebuild. Whatever the program was in the middle of doing, it keeps doing. That isn't a development convenience bolted on the side. It's how the app loads its interface in the first place — which matters later, when something other than a person starts editing that file.
